# Sources And Provenance

This file identifies the works meant by the two familiar English titles and
records which editions are safe and useful as research sources.

## Annals Of A Fortress

### Original work

- Author and illustrator: Eugène-Emmanuel Viollet-le-Duc
- French title: *Histoire d'une forteresse*
- Original publisher: J. Hetzel, Paris
- Original publication: 1874
- Form: illustrated didactic history of an ideal fortress
- French digital source: Bibliothèque nationale de France / Gallica,
  <https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k36156x>

The original French edition describes itself as text and drawings by
Viollet-le-Duc and includes color plates. It is the best authority when an
English technical term or place name seems ambiguous.

### English edition used here

- English title: *Annals of a Fortress*
- Translator: Benjamin Bucknall, architect
- English publication represented by the scan: James R. Osgood and Company,
  Boston, 1876
- Project Gutenberg ebook: number 41426
- Illustrated HTML:
  <https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41426/pg41426-images.html>
- Catalog and downloads: <https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/41426>

Project Gutenberg produced its edition from scans made available by the
Internet Archive. Its HTML is particularly useful because the prose, chapter
structure, technical glossary, and numbered illustrations can be linked and
searched together.

### Rights and handling

The original and Bucknall translation are public-domain works in the United
States. A future project may preserve a source snapshot when a concrete
executable analysis needs stable text or images. Until then, this hub records
the authoritative locations rather than carrying a redundant raw copy.

When citing the book in model research, record:

- edition and language;
- chapter;
- printed page when available;
- figure number;
- whether a measurement came from text, a plate, or a project reconstruction.

## Castles And Warfare In The Middle Ages

### Original work and translation

The modern Dover title is an edition of an older work:

- Author: Eugène-Emmanuel Viollet-le-Duc
- French title: *Essai sur l'architecture militaire au Moyen Âge*
- French publication: Bance, Paris, 1854
- English title: *An Essay on the Military Architecture of the Middle Ages*
- Translator: M. Macdermott, architect
- English publisher: J. H. and J. Parker, Oxford and London
- English publication: 1860
- Length of the 1860 edition: 274 pages
- Google Books record and scan:
  <https://books.google.com/books?id=jmeTa9bJclwC>

The 1860 edition states that it includes the original French engravings. It is
the public-domain textual source behind the later title.

### Dover edition

- Modern title: *Castles and Warfare in the Middle Ages*
- Publisher: Dover Publications, Mineola, New York
- Paperback publication: 2005
- Paperback ISBN: 978-0-486-44020-0
- Ebook ISBN: 978-0-486-13757-5
- Publisher page:
  <https://store.doverpublications.com/products/9780486440200>

The Dover edition is convenient for reading, indexing, and referring to the
work by its familiar modern title. Page numbers and editorial matter may not
match the 1860 edition.

### Rights and handling

The 1854 French work and 1860 English translation are public domain in the
United States. Dover's modern typesetting, cover, and added editorial material
should not be assumed to be public domain. Do not commit a purchased Dover PDF
or ebook to a repository.

If stable source text or plates become necessary, derive them from a verified
public-domain scan of the 1854 or 1860 edition and document the exact source.
Keep personal purchased files and temporary OCR outside the repository until
a clean, lawful, reproducible source artifact is prepared.

## Terminology And Edition Differences

The translations preserve archaic spellings and sometimes use English terms
whose modern meanings are narrower or different. Likely trouble spots include:

- barbican and barbacane;
- keep and donjon;
- bailey, enceinte, and cité;
- boulevard as an artillery outwork rather than a modern street;
- braie and fausse-braie;
- crenelle, battlement, and embrasure;
- fire artillery as an early term for gunpowder artillery;
- command as geometric dominance or exposure, not merely authority.

Barbacane should preserve the source term in citations while giving the model
concept a precise project definition. A glossary entry should state whether a
term is quoted, normalized, or deliberately redefined.

## Source Reliability Labels

Use four labels in derived notes and executable reports:

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Source fact | Directly stated or depicted in an identified edition |
| Interpretation | A reasoned reading of what the source means |
| Model assumption | A simplification introduced to make the world executable |
| Generated finding | A result computed from the encoded scenario |

A source plate is evidence of Viollet-le-Duc's proposed or reconstructed
geometry. It is not automatically evidence that a real historical structure
had exactly those dimensions or effects.
