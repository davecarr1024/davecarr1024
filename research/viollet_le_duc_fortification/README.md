# Viollet-le-Duc Fortification Books

This collection treats two works by Eugène-Emmanuel Viollet-le-Duc as a paired
research foundation:

- *Annals of a Fortress* supplies one persistent place, Val d'Ohet, whose
  defenses are repeatedly rebuilt and tested as attackers, weapons, political
  conditions, and military doctrine change.
- *Castles and Warfare in the Middle Ages* supplies the comparative technical
  vocabulary behind those transformations: sites, walls, towers, keeps,
  gates, outworks, mines, artillery, field works, and overlapping defense.

Together they support a durable project question:

> Given ground, an objective, available resources, contemporary means, and an
> adversary model, can an engineer produce and explain a defensible plan?

Barbacane is the first direct project expression of that question. The books
also support reusable work on analytical geometry, executable reports,
constraint-guided design, adversarial search, and explanations derived from
structured evidence.

## Why These Books Belong Together

The books have complementary forms.

| Book | Form | Principal contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Annals of a Fortress* | Illustrated narrative through successive eras | A persistent world, decisions under pressure, and consequences over time |
| *Castles and Warfare in the Middle Ages* | Comparative architectural and military essay | A catalog of mechanisms, examples, transformations, and technical vocabulary |

The essay explains what a keep, barbican, mine, projecting tower, gun
embrasure, or earth rampart is trying to accomplish. The chronicle shows how
such claims fare when an intelligent enemy responds. One is closer to a
reference manual; the other is closer to an executable scenario suite.

## Research Posture

These are nineteenth-century works of synthesis and argument, not neutral
modern datasets. Viollet-le-Duc reconstructs incomplete evidence, selects
examples to support a rational evolutionary account, and writes with the
concerns of France before and after the Franco-Prussian War.

Use the books confidently for:

- design principles and adversarial questions;
- period vocabulary that can become explicit model rules;
- illustrated explanation patterns;
- historically inspired scenario structure;
- hypotheses worth turning into geometry, constraints, and tests.

Do not use them alone for:

- precise archaeological fact;
- universal claims about every region or century;
- calibrated weapon performance;
- labor, cost, range, or damage values;
- modern judgments about the historical effectiveness of a surviving work.

Those uses require modern corroboration. Barbacane should label a claim as a
source observation, project interpretation, model assumption, or generated
result rather than allowing the categories to blur.

## Ownership

This collection remains in the hub as a shared book foundation. Once
Barbacane has a repository, its source-specific scenario reconstructions,
measurements, extracted plates, experiments, and model findings should live in
Barbacane's own research directory. This directory should retain the durable
book synthesis and cross-project vocabulary, not a second working copy of the
project's research tree.
