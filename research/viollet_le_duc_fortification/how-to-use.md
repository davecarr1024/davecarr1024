# How To Use This Research

Use these books as an engineering lens, not as a requirements dump or a source
of decorative medieval vocabulary.

## If Shaping Barbacane

Read in this order:

1. [shared-themes.md](shared-themes.md)
2. [barbacane-design-support.md](barbacane-design-support.md)
3. The relevant book note and source chapter
4. [sources.md](sources.md) before capturing text, plates, or dimensions

Promote a book detail into the project when it becomes at least one of:

- a scenario input;
- a legal construction or action;
- a geometric predicate;
- an effect model;
- a resource constraint;
- an agent decision;
- a structured claim or diagnostic;
- a diagram layer;
- a test fixture or expected relationship.

Leave it in research when it is only atmosphere, an uncorroborated historical
claim, or detail that the current vertical slice cannot exercise.

## If Reconstructing A Figure

Record a small provenance manifest alongside the reconstruction:

- work and edition;
- chapter, page, and figure;
- source image URI or local source identifier;
- crop, rotation, and scale operations;
- control points used for alignment;
- which dimensions are stated and which are inferred;
- uncertainty or alternative readings;
- coordinate system and units used by the model.

Do not silently trace a plate and present the resulting coordinates as source
measurements. The plate may be diagrammatic, distorted by scanning, or drawn
to explain relationships rather than preserve scale.

## If Writing A Report

Keep these voices visibly distinct:

1. Viollet-le-Duc states or depicts something.
2. The project interprets its military purpose.
3. The scenario encodes a simplified rule.
4. The analyzer derives a result from that rule.
5. The report explains the result and its limitations.

Useful language includes:

- "The source depicts..."
- "We interpret this as..."
- "This study assumes..."
- "Under that assumption, the analyzer finds..."
- "The result does not yet account for..."

That wording preserves an evidence chain while allowing the generated report
to read naturally.

## If Building A New Scenario

Start with a commission rather than a catalog of works:

- What ground is available?
- What must be protected?
- What can the attacker do?
- What can the defender build and operate?
- What resources and time are available?
- What attack does the engineer consider most likely?
- What result would count as improvement?

Then choose the smallest work whose tactical effect can be represented,
tested, and explained.

## Citation Practice

Prefer a citation that can be checked against a public-domain edition. Include
the modern Dover title when it is the edition actually read, but cross-reference
the underlying 1860 title when preserving source excerpts or figures.

For derived project material, cite the exact source location and this research
collection. Do not cite a summary here as though it were the primary source.

## Promotion Rule

The shared interpretation stays here. Project-owned source extraction,
scenario geometry, executable fixtures, generated reports, and findings move
to the project's research directory once the project exists.
